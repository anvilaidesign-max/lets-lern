## Why contracts matter

Every sale, job, loan, lease, phone plan and freelance project is a contract. A **contract** is an agreement the law will enforce. Because Zimbabwe's contract law is Roman-Dutch, shared with South Africa, the principles below apply across much of southern Africa.

## What makes a contract valid

A contract is valid when these requirements are met:

- **Agreement (consensus).** The parties genuinely agree on the same thing, usually through an **offer** that is **accepted**.
- **Serious intention** to create legal obligations. A promise to meet a friend for lunch is not a contract.
- **Capacity.** Parties must be legally able to contract. Minors (under 18) generally need a guardian's assistance, and companies act through authorised people.
- **Legality.** The purpose must be lawful: a contract to sell stolen goods is void.
- **Possibility.** Performance must be possible when the contract is made.
- **Formalities**, where the law requires them. Most contracts can be oral, but some, such as a sale of land, must be in writing and signed.

Roman-Dutch law does **not** require **consideration**, meaning something given in return. The South African Appellate Division confirmed this in *Conradie v Rossouw* (1919). A serious promise to give something for free can therefore be enforceable, which is not the case under English law.

## Offer and acceptance

An **offer** is a firm proposal that can be accepted as it stands. Many things that look like offers are only **invitations to treat**, invitations to make an offer: prices in a shop window, most adverts and a menu. When you take goods to the till, you make the offer and the shop accepts it.

**Acceptance** must match the offer exactly. A reply that changes the terms ("yes, but at a lower price") is a **counter-offer**, which kills the original offer. As a general rule, the contract is formed when the offeror learns of the acceptance.

## When agreement is not real

A contract can be set aside if consent was obtained improperly:

- **Misrepresentation:** a false statement of fact that induced the contract, such as a seller lying about a car's mileage.
- **Duress:** consent obtained by threats of harm.
- **Undue influence:** someone exploited a position of influence over a weakened person.
- **Mistake:** in limited cases, a fundamental mistake means there was no real agreement at all.

The innocent party can usually choose to cancel the contract and may also claim damages.

## Terms: what the contract actually says

- **Express terms** are stated in words, spoken or written.
- **Implied terms** are read in by law, such as a seller's guarantee against hidden defects in Roman-Dutch sale law, unless validly excluded.
- **Tacit terms** are ones both parties obviously intended but never said.
- **Conditions** make obligations depend on a future event, such as "subject to the buyer getting a loan".

Courts read contracts as a whole, in context, to find what the parties meant. Unclear wording is often interpreted against the party who drafted it.

## Breach and remedies

A **breach** happens when a party fails to perform, performs late or badly, or says in advance they won't perform (**repudiation**). The innocent party can:

- Claim **specific performance**: a court order to do what was promised. In Roman-Dutch law this is available as of right, though courts can refuse it where it would be unfair or impractical.
- Claim **damages**, to be put in the position they would have been in had the contract been performed. Losses must be caused by the breach and not too remote, and you are expected to limit (mitigate) your loss.
- **Cancel** the contract, if the breach is serious (material) or the contract allows it.

Contracts often include a **penalty or liquidated damages clause** fixing an amount payable on breach, and a **dispute resolution clause** choosing arbitration or mediation over court.

## A checklist for freelancers and founders

Before signing a project or client contract, check:

- **Scope:** exactly what is delivered, and how changes are priced.
- **Payment:** amounts, currency, deadlines, deposits and late-payment interest.
- **Intellectual property:** who owns the code, designs or drawings, and when ownership passes.
- **Liability:** caps on how much each side can claim, and exclusions.
- **Confidentiality** and how long it lasts.
- **Termination:** how either side can exit, and what is paid if they do.
- **Disputes:** which law applies and where disputes are resolved.

Get everything in writing, even when an oral agreement would be legally valid. The main problem in most disputes is proving what was agreed.

# Key points
- A valid contract needs agreement, serious intention, capacity, legality, possibility and any required formalities.
- Roman-Dutch law, used in Zimbabwe, does not require consideration.
- Shop prices and adverts are usually invitations to treat; a counter-offer rejects the original offer.
- Misrepresentation, duress and undue influence let the innocent party cancel.
- Remedies for breach: specific performance, damages and, for serious breaches, cancellation.
