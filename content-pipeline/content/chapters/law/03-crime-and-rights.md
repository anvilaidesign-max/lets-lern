## What makes something a crime

A **crime** is conduct the state prohibits and punishes. Zimbabwe's main criminal law is written down in the **Criminal Law (Codification and Reform) Act [Chapter 9:23]**, which defines most crimes, from theft and fraud to murder, along with the general rules of criminal liability. Other Acts create further offences, for example on road traffic and cybercrime.

Most crimes need proof of two elements:

- **Actus reus**, the guilty act: conduct (or sometimes a failure to act) that the law prohibits, such as taking property or causing death.
- **Mens rea**, the guilty mind: usually **intention**, but for some crimes **negligence** is enough. Culpable homicide, for example, covers negligently causing a death.

The conduct must also be **unlawful**, meaning no legal justification applies. Taking someone's identical bag by mistake is not theft, because the intention to steal is missing.

## Defences

An accused person may escape liability if a defence applies:

- **Private defence (self-defence):** using reasonable force against an unlawful attack. The response must be necessary and proportionate.
- **Necessity:** acting to avoid a greater harm, in limited circumstances.
- **Mental disorder:** someone who could not understand or control their actions because of mental illness may be found not guilty, but can be detained for treatment.
- **Youth:** very young children cannot be held criminally responsible.
- **Mistake** of fact can remove intention.

## After an arrest

The police may arrest someone they reasonably suspect of a crime. Section 50 of the Constitution then protects the arrested person, who must be:

- told **at once why** they are being arrested, in a language they understand;
- allowed to **contact a lawyer** and a relative;
- told they have the **right to remain silent** and not to be forced to confess;
- treated humanely, with **no torture**; and
- brought before a **court within 48 hours**, or released.

Arrested people must be **released**, with or without conditions, unless there are **compelling reasons** to keep them in custody. This is why **bail** is the normal outcome. A court may refuse bail if the accused is likely to run away, interfere with witnesses or commit more crimes.

## Fair trial rights

Section 70 of the Constitution gives every accused person the right to:

- be **presumed innocent** until proven guilty;
- be told of the charge in enough detail to answer it;
- have adequate **time and facilities** to prepare a defence;
- choose a **lawyer**, and have one provided by the state at state expense if substantial injustice would otherwise result;
- a **public trial** within a reasonable time;
- **remain silent**, and not be compelled to give self-incriminating evidence;
- challenge the evidence and cross-examine witnesses;
- an **interpreter** if needed; and
- **appeal** a conviction or sentence to a higher court.

Evidence obtained in breach of these rights, such as a confession extracted by force, must be excluded if using it would make the trial unfair.

## Proof beyond reasonable doubt

The prosecution carries the **burden of proof** and must prove every element beyond reasonable doubt. That does not mean beyond all possible doubt, but if a reasonable doubt remains the accused must be acquitted. The high standard reflects a basic choice: it is better that some guilty people go free than that innocent people are punished.

## Sentencing

After a conviction, the court chooses a **sentence** within the limits set by law, weighing:

- how serious the crime was and the harm caused;
- the offender's circumstances, including age, first offence or not, and remorse;
- the interests of society: deterrence, protection and rehabilitation.

Options include fines, community service, suspended sentences and imprisonment. In **December 2024** Zimbabwe **abolished the death penalty**, replacing it with imprisonment for the most serious crimes.

## Why this matters to everyone

Criminal procedure rights are not technicalities for the guilty: they protect innocent people from wrongful conviction and from abuse of state power. Knowing them helps you stay calm and safe. If you are arrested, stay polite, do not resist, ask why you are being arrested, ask for a lawyer, and do not sign a statement you have not read or do not understand.

# Key points
- Most crimes need a guilty act (actus reus) and a guilty mind (mens rea), and the conduct must be unlawful.
- Zimbabwe's criminal law is mainly codified in the Criminal Law (Codification and Reform) Act.
- On arrest you must be told why, may stay silent, may call a lawyer, and must see a court within 48 hours.
- Bail is the norm unless there are compelling reasons to detain.
- The prosecution must prove guilt beyond reasonable doubt; the accused is presumed innocent.
- Zimbabwe abolished the death penalty in December 2024.
