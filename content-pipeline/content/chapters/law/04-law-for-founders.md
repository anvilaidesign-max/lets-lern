## Why engineers need some law

If you build something valuable, whether a product, a startup or a consulting business, law decides who owns it, who is liable when it fails, and what you may do with other people's data and code. You don't need to be a lawyer, but knowing the key ideas helps you ask the right questions early, when fixing problems is cheap.

## Choosing a business form

- **Sole trader:** you and the business are the same person. It is simple, but you are **personally liable** for every business debt.
- **Partnership:** two or more people share profits and, usually, unlimited liability for the firm's debts.
- **Private company:** a separate **legal person**. In Zimbabwe, companies and **private business corporations (PBCs)**, a simpler form for small businesses, are governed by the Companies and Other Business Entities Act.

Investors almost always expect a company, because shares make ownership easy to divide, transfer and dilute.

## A company is a separate person

A registered company owns its own assets, signs its own contracts, and sues and is sued in its own name. Shareholders have **limited liability**: if the company fails, they lose what they invested, but creditors cannot usually take their houses. The classic authority is the English case *Salomon v Salomon & Co Ltd* (1897), and Roman-Dutch systems follow the same principle.

Courts can **pierce the corporate veil** and hold people personally liable where the company is a sham used for fraud. Directors who trade recklessly while insolvent can also become personally liable.

## Directors' duties

Directors run the company and owe it **fiduciary duties**:

- act in **good faith** in the company's interests;
- avoid **conflicts of interest**, and disclose any personal interest in a deal;
- use powers for their proper purpose;
- act with reasonable **care, skill and diligence**.

As a founder-director, you are not the company. Keep company money separate, record important decisions, and file returns on time.

## Founder agreements

Most startup disputes are between co-founders. Before building together, agree in writing:

- **who owns what percentage**, and what each person contributes;
- **vesting**: shares earned over time (commonly four years with a one-year cliff), so a founder who leaves early does not keep a large stake;
- **IP assignment**: all code, designs and inventions belong to the company, including work done before incorporation;
- roles, decision-making and what happens if someone leaves.

## Protecting what you build

- **Copyright** protects original works such as source code, documentation, designs and drawings. It arises **automatically** when the work is recorded. It protects the expression, not the idea: others can build a similar app, but cannot copy your code.
- **Patents** protect new, inventive and useful technical inventions for about **20 years**, in exchange for publishing how they work. They are territorial, expensive and must be applied for. Showing the invention publicly before filing can destroy its **novelty**, so file first, then publish.
- **Trademarks** protect brand names and logos that distinguish your products. Register them in each market you care about, and check that nobody else has the name first.
- **Trade secrets** protect confidential know-how, such as algorithms, formulas and customer lists, for as long as they stay secret. Use **non-disclosure agreements (NDAs)** and access controls.

**Who owns an employee's work?** Generally, work created by an employee in the course of employment belongs to the employer. Freelancers and contractors usually keep ownership unless the contract assigns it. That is why client contracts must deal with IP explicitly.

## Open-source licences

Open-source code is free to use but not free of conditions:

- **Permissive licences** (MIT, BSD, Apache 2.0) let you use the code in closed commercial products, provided you keep the copyright and licence notices. Apache 2.0 also grants patent rights.
- **Copyleft licences** (GPL) require that software distributed with GPL code be released under the GPL too, including its source code. The AGPL extends this to software offered over a network.

Before shipping a product, list your dependencies and their licences. Investors check this during **due diligence**.

## Data protection

Apps collect personal data: names, phone numbers, locations, photos. Data protection law requires you to:

- have a **lawful basis**, such as consent or contract, for collecting data;
- collect only what you **need**, and use it only for the stated purpose;
- keep it **secure**, and report serious breaches;
- respect people's rights to **access, correct and delete** their data.

Zimbabwe's **Cyber and Data Protection Act** (2021) sets these rules locally, with POTRAZ as the data protection authority. The EU's **GDPR** applies if you serve people in the EU, and its fines can reach 4% of global annual turnover. A clear **privacy policy** and **terms of service** are also required by app stores.

# Key points
- Sole traders and partners risk personal assets; a company is a separate legal person with limited liability.
- Directors owe fiduciary duties: good faith, avoiding conflicts, and care and skill.
- Founder agreements should cover ownership, vesting and IP assignment.
- Copyright is automatic; patents must be filed before public disclosure; trademarks protect brands; trade secrets need confidentiality.
- Permissive open-source licences allow closed products; GPL copyleft requires sharing source when you distribute.
- Data protection law requires a lawful basis, minimal data, security and respect for users' rights.

# Quiz
Q: A company owes money it cannot pay. What do shareholders normally risk?
- Their houses and cars
* Only what they invested in the company
- Nothing at all, not even their shares
- Twice what they invested
> Limited liability caps shareholders' loss at their investment, because the company is a separate legal person.

Q: You invented a new sensor circuit. What should you do before presenting it at a public conference?
- Nothing, copyright protects it automatically
* File a patent application, because public disclosure can destroy novelty
- Register a trademark for the circuit
- Publish the schematic online first
> Patents require novelty. Filing before disclosure keeps the invention patentable.

Q: Which licence could force you to release your own source code if you distribute software containing its code?
- MIT
- Apache 2.0
* GPL
- BSD
> The GPL is a copyleft licence: derivative works you distribute must also be released under the GPL.

Q: Why do investors want co-founders' shares to vest over time?
- To reduce tax
* So a founder who leaves early does not keep a large stake
- Because the law requires it
- To make the company a partnership
> Vesting, commonly four years with a one-year cliff, ties ownership to continued contribution.

Q: Under data protection law, what should an app collect?
- As much data as possible, in case it is useful later
* Only the personal data it needs for a stated purpose, with a lawful basis
- Any data, as long as it is encrypted
- Only data from users over 30
> Data minimisation and a lawful basis are core principles of modern data protection laws.
