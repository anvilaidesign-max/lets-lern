## Atoms and elements

All matter is made of atoms. An atom has a tiny nucleus of protons and neutrons with electrons around it. The number of protons, the **atomic number**, decides the element: 1 is hydrogen, 6 is carbon, 26 is iron, 29 is copper. Atoms of the same element with different numbers of neutrons are **isotopes**; carbon-14, for example, is radioactive and used to date ancient remains.

## The periodic table

In 1869 Dmitri Mendeleev arranged the known elements by mass and chemical behaviour, leaving gaps for elements not yet discovered, and correctly predicted their properties. Today's table, ordered by atomic number, has 118 confirmed elements.

- **Rows (periods)** show how many electron shells an atom has.
- **Columns (groups)** share the same number of outer electrons, so they behave alike. Group 1 metals (sodium, potassium) react violently with water; group 18 noble gases (helium, neon, argon) barely react at all.
- **Metals** on the left conduct electricity and lose electrons easily; **non-metals** on the right tend to gain or share them. **Metalloids** such as silicon sit in between, which is exactly why silicon is useful for semiconductors.

## Chemical bonds

Atoms bond to reach stable arrangements of outer electrons:

- **Ionic bonds:** one atom gives electrons to another, making oppositely charged ions that attract. Table salt (NaCl) is sodium and chloride ions in a crystal.
- **Covalent bonds:** atoms share electron pairs. Water (H₂O), methane (CH₄) and the carbon chains of all living things are covalent.
- **Metallic bonds:** outer electrons roam freely through a lattice of metal ions, which is why metals conduct electricity and heat and can be bent without breaking.

## Chemical reactions

In a reaction, bonds break and new ones form, but atoms are neither created nor destroyed, so equations must balance:

> CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O      (burning methane)

Reactions that release energy are **exothermic** (combustion, the reaction in a battery); those that absorb it are **endothermic** (photosynthesis, dissolving some salts). **Catalysts** speed reactions without being used up: car catalytic converters, industrial fertiliser production and the enzymes in your body all rely on them.

Acids release hydrogen ions and bases accept them; the pH scale measures this, with 7 as neutral. Corrosion (rust) is a slow reaction of iron with oxygen and water, which is why engineers galvanise steel with zinc or paint it.

## The molecules of life

Living things are built mostly from carbon, hydrogen, oxygen and nitrogen. Four families of molecules do most of the work: **carbohydrates** (energy and structure), **lipids** (membranes and energy storage), **proteins** (enzymes, muscles, hormones, antibodies) and **nucleic acids** (DNA and RNA, the information store).

## From DNA to protein

**DNA** is a double helix of two strands built from four bases: adenine (A), thymine (T), guanine (G) and cytosine (C). A always pairs with T and G with C, so each strand is a template for the other, which is how DNA copies itself when cells divide.

A **gene** is a stretch of DNA that codes for a protein. The **central dogma** of molecular biology describes the flow of information: DNA is **transcribed** into messenger RNA, which is **translated** by ribosomes into a chain of amino acids that folds into a working protein. Three bases (a codon) specify each amino acid. A single changed letter, a **mutation**, can alter a protein: sickle cell disease is caused by one such change in the gene for haemoglobin.

## Cells and evolution

The **cell** is the basic unit of life. Bacteria are simple cells without a nucleus; plant and animal cells are larger, with a nucleus and organelles such as mitochondria.

In 1859 Charles Darwin published *On the Origin of Species*, describing evolution by **natural selection**: individuals vary, some variations help survival and reproduction, and those variations become more common over generations. Evolution explains the shared DNA of all life and why bacteria evolve resistance to antibiotics when these drugs are overused: the few resistant bacteria survive and multiply.

# Key points
- The atomic number (protons) decides the element; isotopes differ in neutrons.
- Groups in the periodic table share outer electrons and chemical behaviour.
- Ionic bonds transfer electrons, covalent bonds share them, metallic bonds free them.
- Balanced equations conserve atoms; catalysts speed reactions without being used up.
- DNA → RNA → protein; A pairs with T and G with C.
- Natural selection explains evolution and antibiotic resistance.
