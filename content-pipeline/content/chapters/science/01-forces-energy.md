## Newton's three laws

In 1687 Isaac Newton published the *Principia*, which described motion with three laws that still underpin nearly all engineering.

- **First law (inertia):** an object stays at rest, or keeps moving at constant velocity, unless a net force acts on it. A satellite coasts through space for years without an engine.
- **Second law:** force equals mass times acceleration.
- **Third law:** for every action there is an equal and opposite reaction. A rocket pushes exhaust gas backwards; the gas pushes the rocket forwards.

> F = m × a      (1 newton accelerates 1 kg by 1 m/s²)

A 1200 kg car accelerating at 3 m/s² needs a net force of 3600 N. On Earth, gravity pulls every kilogram with about 9.81 N, so a 70 kg person weighs about 687 N. **Mass** (kg) is the amount of matter; **weight** (N) is the force of gravity on it. On the Moon your mass is the same but your weight is about one sixth.

## Momentum

**Momentum** is mass times velocity (p = m·v). In a collision with no outside forces, total momentum is conserved. A heavy truck and a light car at the same speed have very different momenta, which is why the truck is so much harder to stop. Airbags and crumple zones work by making the stop take longer: the same change in momentum spread over more time means a smaller force.

## Work, energy and power

**Work** is done when a force moves something: W = F × d (joules). **Energy** is the capacity to do work, and it takes many forms:

- **Kinetic energy** (motion): E_k = ½mv². Doubling speed quadruples kinetic energy, which is why speed is so dangerous in crashes.
- **Gravitational potential energy:** E_p = mgh. Lifting 1000 kg of water 50 m stores about 490 kJ, the principle behind hydroelectric dams such as Kariba.
- **Thermal, chemical, electrical, nuclear and light energy.**

**Power** is the rate of doing work: P = E ÷ t, in watts. A 2 kW kettle transfers 2000 joules every second.

## Conservation of energy

Energy cannot be created or destroyed; it only changes form. This is the **first law of thermodynamics**. In a hydro station, potential energy becomes kinetic energy in the water, then rotational energy in the turbine, then electrical energy in the generator.

No conversion is perfect. Some energy always becomes waste heat, which is why **efficiency** matters:

> Efficiency = useful energy out ÷ energy in × 100%

A good electric motor is over 90 percent efficient; a petrol car engine converts only about 20 to 30 percent of its fuel energy into motion.

## Friction and heat

Friction opposes motion and turns kinetic energy into heat. It is a nuisance in bearings and engines (hence lubrication) but essential for walking, braking and belt drives. The **second law of thermodynamics** adds that heat flows naturally from hot to cold, and that useful energy steadily degrades into dispersed heat: the reason no machine can run forever.

## Circular motion and gravity

An object moving in a circle is always accelerating towards the centre, even at constant speed, because its direction keeps changing. Newton's **law of universal gravitation** says every mass attracts every other with a force proportional to the product of the masses and inversely proportional to the square of the distance. The same law explains a falling apple, the Moon's orbit and the path of a communications satellite.

# Key points
- Newton's second law: F = m × a; weight = mass × 9.81 m/s² on Earth.
- Every action has an equal and opposite reaction; momentum p = mv is conserved in collisions.
- Kinetic energy = ½mv²: doubling speed quadruples energy.
- Energy is conserved; it only changes form, and every conversion loses some as heat.
- Power = energy ÷ time; efficiency = useful output ÷ input.
