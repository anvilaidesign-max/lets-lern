## Electric charge

Matter is made of atoms with positive protons and negative electrons. Most objects are neutral, but moving electrons from one to another creates a net **charge**. Like charges repel and unlike charges attract. **Coulomb's law** says the force grows with the charges and falls with the square of the distance, the same inverse-square pattern as gravity, but vastly stronger: the electric force between an electron and a proton is about 10³⁹ times their gravitational attraction.

A charge creates an **electric field** around it. Voltage is the energy per unit charge needed to move against that field. In a conductor such as copper, outer electrons are free to drift, so a voltage pushes them along as a current. In an insulator such as rubber or glass they are bound in place.

## Currents make magnetism

In 1820 Hans Christian Ørsted noticed that a wire carrying current deflected a nearby compass needle. Moving charge creates a **magnetic field**. Wind the wire into a coil and the fields add up into an **electromagnet**, which can be switched on and off. Electromagnets drive relays, contactors, solenoid valves, loudspeakers and MRI scanners.

A current-carrying wire in a magnetic field feels a force (F = B × I × L for a wire at right angles to the field). This is the **motor effect**: every electric motor turns electrical energy into motion this way.

## Faraday's law of induction

In 1831 Michael Faraday discovered the reverse: a **changing** magnetic field induces a voltage in a nearby conductor. The faster the change, the bigger the voltage. Moving a magnet into a coil, or rotating a coil in a magnetic field, generates electricity.

Nearly all the world's electricity comes from this principle: steam, water or wind turns a turbine, which spins a magnet inside coils (or coils inside a magnet) in a **generator**. Coal stations such as Hwange and hydro stations such as Kariba differ only in what turns the turbine.

**Lenz's law** adds the direction: the induced current always opposes the change that caused it. That is why an inductor resists changes in current, and why a strong magnet dropped through a copper pipe falls slowly.

## Transformers

A **transformer** has two coils on a shared iron core. AC in the primary creates a changing magnetic flux, which induces a voltage in the secondary. The voltage ratio equals the turns ratio:

> V_s ÷ V_p = N_s ÷ N_p

Stepping up to hundreds of kilovolts for transmission reduces the current, and power loss in the lines (I²R) falls with the square of the current. Transformers only work with AC, because DC does not produce a changing flux, which is a big reason AC won the "war of the currents".

## Maxwell and electromagnetic waves

In the 1860s James Clerk Maxwell unified electricity and magnetism in four equations. They predicted that a changing electric field creates a magnetic field and vice versa, so the two can sustain each other and travel through empty space as a wave. The predicted speed matched the measured speed of light. **Light is an electromagnetic wave.**

## The electromagnetic spectrum

All electromagnetic waves travel at the speed of light, about 3 × 10⁸ m/s in a vacuum. They differ in frequency f and wavelength λ, linked by c = f × λ. From low to high frequency:

- **Radio waves:** broadcasting, Wi-Fi, mobile networks. A 900 MHz signal has a wavelength of about 33 cm.
- **Microwaves:** radar, satellite links, microwave ovens.
- **Infrared:** heat radiation, TV remote controls, fibre-optic communication.
- **Visible light:** a narrow band from red (about 700 nm) to violet (about 400 nm).
- **Ultraviolet:** causes sunburn; used to sterilise water.
- **X-rays and gamma rays:** high energy, able to pass through soft tissue and damage DNA.

Higher frequency means more energy per photon, which is why UV and X-rays can damage cells while radio waves cannot.

# Key points
- Like charges repel, unlike attract; voltage is energy per unit charge.
- A current creates a magnetic field; a current in a magnetic field feels a force (the motor effect).
- Faraday's law: a changing magnetic field induces a voltage; generators work this way.
- Transformers change AC voltage in proportion to the turns ratio; high voltage cuts transmission losses.
- Light is an electromagnetic wave; all EM waves travel at about 3 × 10⁸ m/s and c = f × λ.
