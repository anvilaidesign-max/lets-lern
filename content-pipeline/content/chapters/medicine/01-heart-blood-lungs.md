## A double pump

The heart is a muscle about the size of a fist that beats around 100,000 times a day. It is really two pumps side by side, each with an upper chamber (**atrium**) that receives blood and a lower chamber (**ventricle**) that pushes it out:

- The **right side** receives oxygen-poor blood from the body and pumps it to the lungs.
- The **left side** receives oxygen-rich blood from the lungs and pumps it to the whole body. Its wall is thicker because it has to push harder.

**Valves** between the chambers and at the exits act like one-way doors. The "lub-dub" sound of a heartbeat is the valves closing.

## The heart's own electricity

Each beat starts with an electrical signal from the **sinoatrial (SA) node**, the heart's natural pacemaker. The signal spreads across the atria, pauses briefly at the atrioventricular node so the ventricles can fill, then races through the ventricles, which contract together. An **ECG** records this electrical activity; doctors read its waves to spot rhythm problems and heart damage. An artificial pacemaker takes over when the natural system fails.

A normal adult resting heart rate is about 60 to 100 beats per minute; fit athletes are often lower.

## Blood vessels and blood

**Arteries** carry blood away from the heart under high pressure; **veins** return it at low pressure, with valves that stop it flowing backwards; **capillaries**, thinner than a hair, connect them, and this is where oxygen and nutrients pass into the tissues.

Blood contains:

- **Red blood cells**, full of haemoglobin, which carries oxygen.
- **White blood cells**, which fight infection.
- **Platelets**, which help blood clot.
- **Plasma**, the liquid that carries nutrients, hormones and waste.

## Blood pressure

Blood pressure is written as two numbers in millimetres of mercury, such as 120/80 mmHg. The first (**systolic**) is the pressure when the heart contracts; the second (**diastolic**) is the pressure when it relaxes between beats. The WHO considers blood pressure of 140/90 or higher on repeated readings to be **hypertension**.

High blood pressure usually causes no symptoms, which is why it is called a "silent killer", yet it strains the heart and damages arteries over years, raising the risk of heart attack, stroke and kidney disease. Salt reduction, exercise, not smoking, limited alcohol and medication when needed all help. The only way to know is to measure it.

## The lungs

Air travels down the windpipe (**trachea**), which splits into two **bronchi** and then into smaller and smaller tubes, ending in about 300 to 500 million tiny air sacs called **alveoli**. Their walls are one cell thick and wrapped in capillaries. Oxygen diffuses from the air into the blood, and carbon dioxide diffuses out to be breathed away. Spread flat, the alveoli would cover roughly the area of a tennis court.

Breathing is driven mainly by the **diaphragm**, a dome-shaped muscle below the lungs. Your brain adjusts breathing mainly in response to carbon dioxide levels, not oxygen.

## When things go wrong

- **Heart attack (myocardial infarction):** a blocked coronary artery starves part of the heart muscle of oxygen. Typical signs include chest pain or pressure that may spread to the arm, jaw or back, shortness of breath and sweating. It is an emergency: call for help at once.
- **Stroke:** a blocked or burst blood vessel in the brain. Remember **FAST**: Face drooping, Arm weakness, Speech difficulty, Time to call emergency services.
- **Asthma:** the airways narrow and become inflamed, causing wheeze and breathlessness, usually treated with inhalers.
- **Anaemia:** too little haemoglobin, causing tiredness and pallor; iron deficiency and malaria are common causes in southern Africa.

Most heart disease is linked to risk factors people can change: smoking, high blood pressure, diabetes, high cholesterol, inactivity and obesity.

# Key points
- The right heart pumps blood to the lungs; the left heart pumps it to the body.
- The SA node is the natural pacemaker; an ECG records the heart's electrical activity.
- Arteries carry blood from the heart; veins return it; capillaries exchange oxygen and nutrients.
- Blood pressure is systolic/diastolic; 140/90 or above on repeated readings is hypertension.
- Gas exchange happens in the alveoli; the diaphragm drives breathing.
- Stroke warning signs: FAST (face, arm, speech, time).
